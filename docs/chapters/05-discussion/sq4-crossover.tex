\section{Discussion of SQ4 --- The Crossover Point}
\label{sec:discuss-sq4}

\textbf{SQ4 asks:} At what concurrency level does the Kubernetes overhead become smaller than the VM's contention-induced degradation --- the crossover point?

\paragraph{Reliability crossover occurs at L4: the primary crossover point.}
The reliability crossover --- the concurrency level at which the VM's success rate first falls below 95\,\% while Kubernetes maintains 100\,\% --- occurred at \textbf{L4 (5 concurrent jobs)}.
This is the primary crossover point for production decision-making: at five concurrent Dagster jobs, the VM already fails one in three runs (66.7\,\%) due to memory exhaustion, while Kubernetes completes all runs successfully within its per-pod limits.

\paragraph{K8s overhead is substantial and grows with load.}
Container startup overhead grows from $\approx$19.5\,s at L1 to $\approx$56.6\,s at L6 (mean across levels: $\approx$40\,s), representing 22--59\,\% of the 86--107\,s total K8s pod time.
Startup overhead increases with concurrency as more containers initialise simultaneously on the single-node cluster, competing for PostgreSQL connections and Python interpreter init.

\paragraph{The performance crossover is not reached within L1--L6.}
In terms of cost per successfully delivered job (accounting for VM failures as re-execution costs):
K8s is more expensive at low concurrency (L1: K8s total 86.3\,s vs VM effective 66.8\,s, a +25\,\% cost).
However, as the VM fails at L4+, its effective cost explodes: at L4, VM effective = 252.8\,s (168.6\,s / 0.667) vs K8s total = estimated $>$168\,s + overhead $>$192\,s.
The performance crossover (K8s total < VM effective) occurs between L1 and L4, suggesting K8s becomes performance-competitive before L4.
At L4--L6, the VM effective time continues growing to 252--undefined while K8s total stabilises at $\approx$92--107\,s.
The performance and reliability crossovers both favour K8s, but the reliability crossover is the binding constraint.

\paragraph{The crossover analysis: net beneficial at L4.}
At L4, both conditions for migration are met:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item VM success rate falls below 95\,\% (66.7\,\%),
  \item K8s effective cost per successful job is lower than the VM's in expectation once failures are factored in.
\end{enumerate}
The net beneficial crossover is therefore at \textbf{L4 (5 concurrent jobs)}, and the binding constraint is \emph{reliability}: the VM is no longer acceptable even before K8s becomes faster per se.

\paragraph{Node-level observations at L6.}
Both environments experience increasing overhead at L6 but from different mechanisms.
The VM fails entirely at L6 (0\% success), delivering zero jobs.
Kubernetes maintained 100\% reliability at L6, with total wall-clock time of $\approx$107\,s (execution $\approx$51\,s + startup $\approx$56.6\,s).
The K8s reliability advantage over the VM is decisive and holds across the entire L1--L6 range at this hardware configuration.

\paragraph{The crossover is hardware-configuration dependent.}
The observed crossover at L3 is specific to this configuration:
\begin{itemize}
  \item 4\,vCPU, 4\,GB RAM VM with 2\,GB per-container Docker limit
  \item 4\,vCPU, 8\,GB Kind node with 2\,GiB per-pod memory limit
  \item 60-second two-phase workload with 400\,MB memory allocation per job
  \item 24--57\,s Kubernetes startup overhead (local Kind cluster, pre-pulled images, single node)
\end{itemize}
If the VM had less RAM, the crossover would occur at a lower concurrency level.
In a production K8s cluster (GKE, EKS) with cold image pulls, startup overhead would be higher (20--60\,s additional), potentially shifting the total K8s cost while the reliability crossover remains determined by VM memory exhaustion.

\paragraph{Consistency with the hypothesis.}
The pre-experiment hypothesis (Section~\ref{sec:hypothesis}) predicted that at low concurrency the VM outperforms Kubernetes due to startup overhead, and at high concurrency Kubernetes outperforms the VM.
The data partly confirms this: at L1, the VM has a small execution time advantage.
However, the hypothesis underestimated how early VM reliability would collapse (L3, not L5--L7) and the significance of K8s startup overhead (24--57\,s, not a few seconds).
The main finding is that despite substantial startup overhead, K8s becomes net beneficial at L3 because VM reliability collapses so sharply.

\paragraph{What the crossover means in practice.}
Teams running Dagster with fewer than 3 concurrent jobs on a well-provisioned VM experience negligible reliability difference and may use either environment.
Teams expecting 3 or more concurrent jobs should migrate to Kubernetes: K8s adds 24--57\,s per-run startup overhead (growing with concurrency) while providing 100\,\% reliability versus the VM's 66.7\,\% or lower success rates at these concurrency levels.
The migration decision threshold is 3 concurrent jobs, and given the clear reliability benefit, earlier migration is also rational for teams anticipating growth in workload concurrency.
