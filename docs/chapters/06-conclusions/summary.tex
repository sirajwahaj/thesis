\section{Summary of Findings}
\label{sec:summary}

This thesis conducted three experiments to empirically determine the concurrency level at which migrating Dagster workflow orchestration from a single VM to Kubernetes becomes net beneficial.

\paragraph{Experiment 1 --- VM degradation profile (SQ1).}
The VM's success rate degraded sharply with concurrency: 100\,\% at L1--L3, then 66.7\,\% at L4, 61.9\,\% at L5, and 0.0\,\% at L6.
The primary failure mechanism was memory exhaustion: five concurrent containers each allocating 400\,MB exceeded the available headroom on the 4\,GB VM, triggering Linux OOM kills.
Execution time of \emph{surviving} jobs increased dramatically once failures began: stable at 66--69\,s for L1--L3 but jumping to 168.6\,s at L4 and 464.1\,s at L5.
The formal reliability threshold (success rate $<$95\%) was crossed between L3 and L4, placing the VM reliability crossover at \textbf{5 concurrent jobs}.

\paragraph{Experiment 2 --- Kubernetes isolation (SQ2).}
Kubernetes maintained 100\,\% job success rate across all concurrency levels (L1--L6), in stark contrast to the VM's collapse from L4 onward.
Execution time variance was dramatically lower on Kubernetes than on the VM at every level.
Container startup overhead (pod creation $\rightarrow$ run completion minus Dagster-measured execution time) ranged from 19.5\,s at L1 to 56.6\,s at L6, growing with concurrency due to concurrent Python initialisation and PostgreSQL connection contention on the single-node Kind cluster.
Blast radius containment tests demonstrated that pod-level cgroup isolation prevents cross-job interference even when pods run concurrently on the same Kind node.

\paragraph{Experiment 3 --- Overhead and crossover (SQ3, SQ4).}
Kubernetes pod scheduling latency was $<$1\,s in the local Kind cluster.
Container startup overhead ranged from 19.5\,s per job at L1 to 56.6\,s at L6, growing with concurrency due to concurrent initialisation contention on the single-node cluster.
The \emph{reliability} crossover occurs at \textbf{L4 (5 concurrent jobs)}: this is the first concurrency level where the VM fails to achieve 95\,\% success rate while Kubernetes maintains 100\,\%.
The \emph{performance} crossover---where total K8s wall-clock time falls below VM effective time per successful delivery---also occurs at L4 once VM contention and failure costs are factored in.
