\section{Summary of Findings}
\label{sec:summary}

This thesis conducted three experiments to empirically determine the concurrency level at which migrating Dagster workflow orchestration from a single VM to Kubernetes becomes net beneficial.

\paragraph{Experiment 1 --- VM degradation profile (SQ1).}
The VM's success rate degraded sharply with concurrency: 100\,\% at L1--L2, then 66.7\,\% at L3, 40.0\,\% at L4, 38.1\,\% at L5, and 30.0\,\% at L6.
The primary failure mechanism was memory exhaustion: three concurrent containers each allocating 400\,MB exceeded the available headroom on the 4\,GB VM, triggering Linux OOM kills.
Execution time of \emph{surviving} jobs varied considerably across levels (66.7\,s at L1--L2, 92.9\,s at L3, peaking at 181.6\,s at L4 due to contention with dying containers), revealing that surviving jobs compete for the same resources as the failing ones.
The formal reliability threshold (success rate $<$95\%) was crossed between L2 and L3, placing the VM reliability crossover at \textbf{3 concurrent jobs}.

\paragraph{Experiment 2 --- Kubernetes isolation (SQ2).}
Kubernetes maintained 100\,\% job success rate across all concurrency levels (L1--L6), in stark contrast to the VM's collapse from L3 onward.
Execution time variance was dramatically lower on Kubernetes than on the VM at every level.
Container startup overhead (pod creation $\rightarrow$ run completion minus Dagster-measured execution time) ranged from 4.3\,s at L1 to 14.9\,s at L6, growing with concurrency due to concurrent Python initialisation and PostgreSQL connection contention on the single-node Kind cluster.
At L1, this represents $\approx$7\% of job execution time; at L6, $\approx$18\%.
Blast radius containment tests (Exp 2B, L4: 5 concurrent jobs) demonstrated that VM resource-contention failures cascade (40.0\,\% success rate) while Kubernetes pod-level cgroup isolation fully prevents cross-job interference (100\,\% success rate).

\paragraph{Experiment 3 --- Overhead and crossover (SQ3, SQ4).}
Kubernetes pod scheduling latency was $<$1\,s in the local Kind cluster.
Container startup overhead ranged from 4.3\,s per job at L1 to 14.9\,s at L6, growing with concurrency due to concurrent initialisation contention on the single-node cluster.
The \emph{reliability} crossover occurs at \textbf{L3 (3 concurrent jobs)}: this is the first concurrency level where the VM fails to achieve 95\,\% success rate while Kubernetes maintains 100\,\%.
The \emph{performance} crossover---where K8s total wall-clock time per successful delivery falls below the VM's effective delivery cost (accounting for reruns)---also occurs at \textbf{L3}: VM effective time = 139.3\,s (92.9\,s / 0.667) vs.\  K8s total = 69.7\,s, a 70\,s advantage per successful job.
Both crossovers aligning at L3 provides an unambiguous answer to SQ4.
