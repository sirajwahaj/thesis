\section{Experiment 3 Results --- Overhead and Crossover Point (SQ3, SQ4)}
\label{sec:results-exp3}

Experiment~3 is derived from the Experiment~1 and~2A datasets: no new runs are required.
Pod scheduling latency and container startup time are extracted from the pod lifecycle timestamps collected during Experiment~2A.
Net execution time differences between the two environments are computed from the respective dagster run timestamps.

\paragraph{Pod scheduling latency.}
In a single-node Kind cluster with pre-pulled images and no resource quotas that could cause pending states, all pods were scheduled within less than 1\,second.
This is consistent with the theoretical minimum for a lightly loaded local cluster.

\paragraph{Container startup time.}
The dominant overhead component is \emph{container startup time}: the interval from pod submission until the Dagster job process begins executing.
This is measured as total pod lifetime (submitted\_ts to container exit) minus the Dagster execution duration (\texttt{end\_time} $-$ \texttt{start\_time} from dagster\_runs.csv), joined on \texttt{run\_id}.
At L1 this yields 4.3\,s, rising to 14.9\,s at L6.
This growth reflects increased Python initialisation queuing and PostgreSQL handshake contention when many containers start concurrently on the single-node cluster.

Note that the pod scheduling latency itself is essentially zero ($<$1\,s) on a single-node Kind cluster with pre-pulled images, so the startup overhead is dominated by container initialisation: Python interpreter start, dependency import, and the Dagster gRPC handshake with the code server.
This contrasts with the $\approx$25\,s median scheduling latency reported by \citet{choi2021} for production GKE clusters where multi-node scheduling decisions dominate.

\paragraph{Reliability crossover (Metric 11).}
The VM's job success rate declined sharply from 100\,\% at L1--L2 to 66.7\,\% at L3, 46.7\,\% at L4, 42.9\,\% at L5, and 30.0\,\% at L6.
The \emph{reliability crossover} threshold --- defined as the concurrency level at which VM success rate drops below 95\% --- was crossed between L2 (100\%) and L3 (66.7\%).
This places the reliability crossover at \textbf{L3 (3 concurrent jobs)}.
This result directly answers SQ1: the VM begins to degrade significantly at 3 concurrent jobs.

\paragraph{Performance crossover (Metric 12).}
The performance crossover is more nuanced than a simple monotone comparison.
K8s execution time itself \emph{increases} with concurrency---from 63.4\,s (L1) to 80.6\,s (L5)---because the single-node Kind cluster experiences node-level resource contention at higher concurrency despite per-pod limits.
Adding the measured startup overhead (4--15\,s, growing with concurrency), K8s total wall-clock is:
L1 $\approx$68\,s, L2 $\approx$69\,s, L3 $\approx$70\,s, L4 $\approx$75\,s, L5 $\approx$92\,s, L6 $\approx$97\,s.

Compared to VM survivor-only execution times, K8s is \emph{competitive or faster} at L2 ($-$7.5\,s), L3 ($-$0.2\,s), L4 ($-$1.4\,s), but \emph{slower} at L5 ($+$14.5\,s), L6 ($+$16.6\,s) and L1 ($+$2.4\,s).
The raw wall-clock performance crossover (K8s faster than VM) is thus at L2--L4; at L5 K8s survivors take longer in this local Kind environment due to node memory pressure at $7 \times \approx 1\,\text{GB} \approx 7\,\text{GB}$ on an 8\,GB node.
However, the VM's 42.9\,\% success rate at L5 means that 57\% of jobs are failing outright---a comparison of survivor execution times is misleading when over half the runs fail.

\paragraph{Combined crossover point.}
The combined crossover --- the concurrency level where Kubernetes becomes net beneficial considering both reliability and execution time --- is \textbf{L3 (3 concurrent jobs)}.
At this level: (1) the VM drops below 95\,\% success rate (66.7\,\%) while Kubernetes maintains 100\,\%, and (2) when the VM failure rate is factored in as a re-execution cost, the VM effective time per successful delivery ($\approx$105\,s at L3) already exceeds the K8s total wall-clock ($\approx$70\,s), making K8s faster on a per-successful-job basis.
The 4--15\,s pre-execution startup overhead per run represents 7--18\,\% of total K8s time; however, this cost is outweighed at L3+ by the VM's inability to reliably complete jobs at all.
At higher concurrency (L4--L5), K8s continues to deliver 100\,\% reliability despite pod startup overhead growing to 7--12\,s; the VM continues to fail at 43--47\,\% rates.
Both environments experience node-level pressure at L6 (10 concurrent jobs) on this 8\,GB single-node Kind cluster, though Kubernetes maintained 100\,\% reliability.
