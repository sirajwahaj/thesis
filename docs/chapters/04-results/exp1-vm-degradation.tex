\subsection{Observed Degradation Pattern}
\label{subsec:exp1-degradation}

Two distinct phases are visible in the results:

\paragraph{Phase 1 --- Reliable operation (L1--L2).}
At one and two concurrent jobs, all launched runs complete successfully.
Mean execution time is 66.7\,s (L1) and 67.3\,s (L2) --- virtually unchanged, confirming that two concurrent jobs on a 4\,vCPU VM do not yet produce significant resource contention.
L2 exhibits low standard deviation (1.3\,s), consistent with both jobs receiving sufficient resources.

\paragraph{Phase 2 --- OOM-driven failure (L3--L6).}
Beginning at three concurrent jobs, a fraction of jobs fail with short durations (37--50\,s) --- midway through the \texttt{memory\_pressure} op.
Inspection of individual run records reveals that failing jobs terminate at 37--50\,s, consistent with an OOM kill occurring within 7--20\,s of the memory allocation call in \texttt{memory\_pressure}.
The failure mechanism is \textbf{memory exhaustion}: with $n$ containers each allocating 400\,MB simultaneously, the aggregate demand quickly approaches or exceeds the 4\,GB VM RAM, triggering the Linux OOM killer which terminates one or more containers.

The success rate drops sharply from 100\,\% to 66.7\,\% between L2 and L3, and continues declining:
\begin{itemize}
  \item L3 (3 jobs): 6/9 = 66.7\,\%
  \item L4 (5 jobs): 6/15 = 40.0\,\%
  \item L5 (7 jobs): 8/21 = 38.1\,\%
  \item L6 (10 jobs): 9/30 = 30.0\,\%
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Execution time of surviving jobs.}
The mean execution time of \emph{successful} runs varies more than expected: 66.7\,s (L1), 67.3\,s (L2), 92.9\,s (L3), 181.6\,s (L4), 92.6\,s (L5), 111.3\,s (L6).
The high mean at L4 (181.6\,s, std 158.7\,s) reflects extreme variance: a few surviving jobs experienced heavy I/O and CPU contention from competing containers still running before OOM kills.
This is a selection effect: only the jobs that received sufficient memory and CPU complete, and at high contention levels the survivors face degraded performance from the OS scheduling the dying containers alongside the surviving ones.

\paragraph{CPU and memory trends.}
System CPU utilisation peaks at L2 (60.8\,\%) and then \emph{decreases} at higher levels (38\,\% at L6).
This counter-intuitive pattern reflects that at high concurrency the majority of jobs are OOM-killed within 40--50\,s, before the CPU-intensive workload contributes to sustained utilisation.
System memory remains elevated throughout (75--82\,\%) due to the combined footprint of the Docker daemon, PostgreSQL, Dagster daemon, and the run containers.

\paragraph{Reliability crossover reached.}
The reliability crossover (VM success rate falling below 95\,\%) occurs between \textbf{L2 and L3}: success rate drops from 100\,\% to 66.7\,\% as the aggregate memory demand first exceeds the VM's available RAM.
This is notably earlier than predicted by the hypothesis, which expected the crossover in the L4--L5 range.


\subsection{Resource Utilisation}
\label{subsec:exp1-resources}

System CPU utilisation peaks at L2 (60.8\,\%) and then decreases at higher levels (38.0\,\% at L6), because at high concurrency the majority of jobs are OOM-killed before completing the CPU-intensive workload.
Memory utilisation follows a similar pattern: each job allocates 400\,MB during its memory-pressure phase, so peak aggregate memory scales as $N \times 400\,\text{MB}$ per level.
At L6, aggregate memory allocation equals the VM's total 4\,GB RAM, explaining the sharp increase in failure rate at that level.

The key SQ1 finding is that the VM exhibited a \textbf{sharp reliability collapse at L3}: success rate dropped from 100\,\% to 66.7\% as aggregate memory demand first exceeded available RAM, and continued declining to 30\,\% at L6. This OOM-driven failure is the primary motivation for evaluating Kubernetes pod isolation.
