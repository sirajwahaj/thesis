\subsection{Observed Degradation Pattern}
\label{subsec:exp1-degradation}

Two distinct phases are visible in the results:

\paragraph{Phase 1 --- Reliable operation (L1--L3).}
At one, two, and three concurrent jobs, all launched runs complete successfully.
Mean execution time is 66.8\,s (L1), 67.4\,s (L2), and 69.0\,s (L3) --- a gradual 3--4\,\% increase attributable to initial CPU contention on the 4\,vCPU VM.
L1--L3 exhibit low standard deviation (0.3--0.8\,s), indicating stable execution with minimal variance during reliable operation.

\paragraph{Phase 2 --- OOM-driven failure (L4--L6).}
Beginning at five concurrent jobs (L4), a fraction of jobs fail with short durations (midway through the \texttt{memory\_pressure} op).
The failure mechanism is \textbf{memory exhaustion}: with $n$ containers each allocating 400\,MB simultaneously, the aggregate demand quickly approaches or exceeds the 4\,GB VM RAM, triggering the Linux OOM killer which terminates one or more containers.

The success rate drops sharply from 100\,\% to 66.7\,\% between L3 and L4, and continues declining:
\begin{itemize}
  \item L4 (5 jobs): 10/15 = 66.7\,\%
  \item L5 (7 jobs): 13/21 = 61.9\,\%
  \item L6 (10 jobs): 0/30 = 0.0\,\%
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Execution time of surviving jobs.}
The mean execution time of \emph{successful} runs increases substantially at high concurrency: 66--69\,s (L1--L3) but jumps to 168.6\,s (L4) and 464.1\,s (L5).
This increase reflects that when the OOM killer begins terminating jobs, the surviving jobs must wait longer for memory resources.
The standard deviation among successful jobs ranges from 0.3\,s (L1--L3) to 147.4\,s (L5), indicating high variance in execution time once failures begin.

\paragraph{CPU and memory trends.}
System CPU utilisation peaks at L2 (60.8\,\%) and then decreases at higher levels (38\,\% at L6).
This counter-intuitive pattern reflects that at high concurrency the majority of jobs are OOM-killed early, before the CPU-intensive workload contributes to sustained utilisation.
System memory remains elevated throughout (75--82\,\%) due to the combined footprint of the Docker daemon, PostgreSQL, Dagster daemon, and the run containers.

\paragraph{Reliability crossover reached.}
The reliability crossover (VM success rate falling below 95\,\%) occurs between \textbf{L3 and L4}: success rate drops from 100\,\% to 66.7\,\% as the aggregate memory demand first exceeds the VM's available RAM.
This is notably earlier than predicted by the hypothesis, which expected the crossover in the L5--L7 range.


\subsection{Resource Utilisation}
\label{subsec:exp1-resources}

System CPU utilisation peaks at L2 (60.8\,\%) and then decreases at higher levels (38.0\,\% at L6), because at high concurrency the majority of jobs are OOM-killed before completing the CPU-intensive workload.
Memory utilisation follows a similar pattern: each job allocates 400\,MB during its memory-pressure phase, so peak aggregate memory scales as $N \times 400\,\text{MB}$ per level.
At L6, aggregate memory allocation equals the VM's total 4\,GB RAM, explaining the complete failure rate at that level.

The key SQ1 finding is that the VM exhibited a \textbf{sharp reliability collapse at L4}: success rate dropped from 100\,\% to 66.7\% as aggregate memory demand first exceeded available RAM, and continued declining to 0\,\% at L6. This OOM-driven failure is the primary motivation for evaluating Kubernetes pod isolation.
